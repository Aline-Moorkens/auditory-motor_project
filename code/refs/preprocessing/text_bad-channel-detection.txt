Automatic bad-channel detection was performed using amplitude-based metrics (root mean square and peak-to-peak amplitude) computed across continuous data. Channels with abnormally high RMS values were identified using robust statistics (median and median absolute deviation), and channels exhibiting flatline activity were excluded. This approach follows established EEG preprocessing pipelines that employ variance-based outlier detection and flatline criteria to identify poor-quality electrodes (Nolan et al., 2010; Bigdely-Shamlo et al., 2015; Oostenveld et al., 2011). Based on the aforementioned automated pipeline and a subsequent visual inspection, recordings from bad channels of each subject were removed.
